# Reviewed Krenko strategy investigation

This is an independent review of a hypothetical pool, not an assessment of a supplied deck or a simulation. “Low budget / power3” specifies a test context, not a price fact or official Commander bracket. No private generated deck, repository source or credentials were sent externally.

## Finding

Role tags and minimum counts describe coverage, not whether the selected cards form a functioning strategy. The next experiment should isolate **package-aware marginal utility under resource and timing constraints**. Avoid replacing one generic ranking bonus with another tribal-name bonus.

## Oracle-grounded distinctions

- Krenko creates Goblins according to Goblins controlled at resolution. Impact Tremors converts creature entries into damage to each opponent; Bombardment converts sacrificed creatures into targeted damage. Neither is interchangeable with a generic creature slot. Chirurgeon supplies a Goblin body plus regeneration purchased with another Goblin resource; it does not protect against exile, sacrifice or effects that prohibit regeneration.
- Electromancer is a three-mana Human Wizard with one-shot party-dependent mana. Alone it contributes Wizard; with Krenko, Warrior is also represented; adding Havoc Raiser provides Rogue. Plain Goblin tokens add none of those classes. Thus test1/2/3 distinct roles rather than inventing “ramp per Goblin.” The publisher confirms the Oracle ability and one-creature-per-party-role rules. [Wizards Zendikar Rising release notes](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/zendikar-rising-release-notes-2020-09-10)
- Bearer costs eight mana, requires dying, and schedules destruction of all permanents for the next end step. It may destroy the token engine, mana and payoffs being built. This is a conditional reset plan requiring demonstrated access and recovery—not automatically uncastable or valid only alongside a particular named outlet.
- Mogg Infestation replaces each creature that actually dies under its targeted player’s control with two Goblins. With Tremors surviving and N creatures actually dying, the token entries produce2N damage to each opponent absent modifying effects. Self-targeting can also destroy Krenko and valuable bodies. Neither “always bad” nor an invented minimum-three-creature threshold follows from Oracle text.
- “Grenzo” must resolve to a specific card. Dungeon Warden costs black/red and is ineligible for mono-red Krenko. Havoc Raiser costs RR; its value depends on connecting in combat, legal timing and mana to cast the exiled spell; exiled lands cannot be played by its permission. [Wizards Commander rules](https://magic.wizards.com/en/formats/commander), [Commander Masters release notes](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/commander-masters-release-notes), [Time Spiral Remastered release notes](https://media.wizards.com/2021/downloads/TSR_Release_Notes/EN_MTGTSR_FAQ_20210118.pdf)

## Falsifiable next tests, in order

1. **Locate the loss before changing scores.** Freeze a legal candidate pool and price snapshot. Track each affordable support option through retrieval, shortlisting, quotas and final optimization with rejection reasons. Required result: every missing candidate has one evidenced cause. An unexplained disappearance fails; surviving retrieval does not prove scoring is sound.
2. **Test mechanical context tables.** Electromancer: Wizard alone=1, Wizard+Warrior=2, plus Rogue=3; extra plain Goblin tokens do not change party. Tremors: zero creature entries=zero trigger damage. Havoc Raiser: no combat connection=no trigger; no available casting resources cannot be scored as guaranteed card gain. Bearer: casting without a death event cannot count as an immediate reset. Pass all exact cases before ranking experiments.
3. **Ablate soft quotas with the pool fixed.** Compare existing quotas against bounded soft targets for creature/utility roles, holding legality, identity, singleton and budget hard. Permit enchantment payoffs to compete for strategic support slots while preserving a declared minimum source/creature capacity. Measure stage exclusions and package completeness. No improvement falsifies the quota-only explanation; it does not by itself identify retrieval as the cause.
4. **Measure package additions and removals.** Test generator alone, payoff alone, and generator+payoff at identical total budgets. Score forecasted reachable entries/sacrifices and resource consumption, including the opportunity cost of losing a Goblin before Krenko resolves. Required result: package benefit disappears when its prerequisite is removed. Ensure a sacrifice-outlet bonus cannot alone justify an eight-mana reset that destroys its own plan.
5. **Compare whole replacements, including mana saved.** Search alternatives at equal or lower total resource demand, not just identical mana values. Preserve unique interaction, protection, token and commander contributions. Compare one-card and bounded two-card swaps; the latter can spend savings on a missing support function. Report which tradeoff makes a swap preferable; reject unexplained weighted-score wins.
6. **Stress the proposed choice.** Run representative board states with commander absent/present, summoning sickness, destruction versus exile, no expendable creatures, blocked combat, mana shortage and a delayed reset. Keep favorable and unfavorable counterexamples. Then evaluate on untouched tribal and nontribal commanders with varied budget/power settings; do not tune on those results and still call them holdouts.

Metrics: supported function coverage; unresolved prerequisites; source/payoff package availability; mana/time until first usable effect; lost resources/functions per proposed swap; evidence-backed counterfactual preference. No simulated win rate or optimal-deck claim is warranted.
